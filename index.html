I work on the reliability of AI that predicts and acts. When should we trust an LLM's decision, or a coding agent's claim that a change is done, and when should the system abstain instead? My main tools are conformal prediction and risk control: methods that give statistical guarantees without assuming how the model works inside.

Current work

  • Decision models under label change. LLM classifiers are calibrated for one set of labels, then the labels change. I study how to keep confidence scores and abstention reliable when the label taxonomy shifts.
  • Quality gates for coding agents. A calibrated check for when a coding agent's "done" can be trusted, with a guaranteed bound on how often a bad change gets through.
  • decoybench. Do AI decision models fall for the decoy effect? An audit of open decision models borrowed from behavioral economics. Release soon.
